# Common Korean English Patterns in American Academic and PhD Settings

This study guide is designed for Korean speakers working or studying in American academic environments, especially CS PhD programs.

The left column is **not always grammatically wrong**. Some expressions are genuine grammar or collocation errors; others are normal English but can sound too direct, vague, overly apologetic, overly absolute, or pragmatically different from what a Korean speaker intends.

The goal is to learn the **full sentence as a reusable pattern**, not just memorize isolated fragments.

**Study method:** Read the left sentence, cover the right column, and produce the natural alternative aloud. Then replace the topic with something from your own research.


## 1. Grammar, Collocations, and Literal Translation
| Common Korean English | Reason | Use Instead |
|---|---|---|
| I discussed about it. | `discuss` does not take `about`. | I discussed it. |
| I researched about retrieval-augmented generation last semester. | `research` usually takes a direct object. | I researched retrieval-augmented generation last semester. |
| I study about distributed systems. | `study` usually takes a direct object. | I study distributed systems. |
| I am interested about language models. | Wrong preposition. | I'm interested in language models. |
| This problem is related with data quality. | Wrong collocation. | This problem is related to data quality. |
| Our approach is similar with the baseline method. | Wrong collocation. | Our approach is similar to the baseline method. |
| Our setting is different with the one used in the paper. | Wrong collocation. | Our setting is different from the one used in the paper. |
| This configuration is same with the previous experiment. | Wrong collocation. | This configuration is the same as the previous experiment. |
| He explained me. | `explain` requires `to` before the person. | He explained it to me. |
| My professor said me that the idea was promising. | `say` cannot be used this way. | My professor told me that the idea was promising. |
| He suggested me to try a larger batch size. | Incorrect verb pattern. | He suggested that I try a larger batch size. |
| I suggest you to compare against the strongest baseline. | Incorrect verb pattern. | I suggest that you compare against the strongest baseline. |
| I recommend you to read this paper before the meeting. | Awkward verb pattern in American English. | I recommend that you read this paper before the meeting. |
| I entered PhD. | Non-native phrasing. | I started my PhD. |
| I entered graduate school. | Understandable, but sounds translated. | I started grad school. |
| I'm first year. | Missing article and noun. | I'm a first-year PhD student. |
| I'm in first year. | Less natural in American English. | I'm in my first year. |
| My senior told me that this conference is very competitive. | `senior` is ambiguous in English. | A senior PhD student in my lab told me that this conference is very competitive. |
| My junior asked me how to run the experiment. | Not normally used as a noun for a less senior student. | A more junior student in my lab asked me how to run the experiment. |
| My lab people usually have lunch together on Fridays. | Non-native phrasing. | People in my lab usually have lunch together on Fridays. |
| I have another schedule. | Korean `스케줄이 있다` does not map naturally. | I have another commitment. / I already have plans. |
| I have a promise. | Korean `약속` is being translated literally. | I have plans. |
| I made a promise with my friend. | Sounds like a serious personal promise. | I made plans with a friend. |
| I will contact you later. | Grammatically correct but stiff. | I'll follow up later. / I'll get back to you. |
| Let's contact later. | Non-native phrasing. | Let's stay in touch. |
| I checked your email. | Can sound like you inspected it. | I saw your email. |
| I checked the paper. | Ambiguous: read, reviewed, or verified? | I took a look at the paper. / I read through the paper. |
| I studied the paper. | Sounds overly literal. | I went through the paper carefully. |
| I saw it in the internet. | Wrong preposition. | I saw it online. |
| I saw a news. | `news` is uncountable. | I saw a news article. |
| I got an information. | `information` is uncountable. | I got some information. |
| I have many experiences. | Usually wrong when referring to accumulated expertise. | I have a lot of experience. |
| I have an experience of working with PyTorch. | Non-native collocation. | I have experience working with PyTorch. |
| I have a career in machine learning. | Often too broad if you simply mean prior work. | I have experience in machine learning. |
| I am confusing. | Means you confuse other people. | I'm confused. |
| I am boring. | Means you are a boring person. | I'm bored. |
| I am stressful. | Means you cause stress. | I'm stressed. |
| I am hard. | Incorrect for “힘들다.” | I'm having a hard time. |
| It is burden. | Literal translation of `부담이다`. | It's a lot to take on. |
| I feel burdened. | Grammatically possible but often unnatural in everyday speech. | I feel a lot of pressure. |
| I have pressure. | Non-native phrasing. | I'm under a lot of pressure. |
| My condition is bad. | Sounds medical or athletic. | I'm not feeling great today. |
| My body is not good. | Non-native phrasing. | I'm not feeling well. |
| I slept late. | Often means you woke up late. | I went to bed late. |
| I slept at 2. | Non-native phrasing. | I went to bed at 2. |
| I took a rest. | Understandable but unnatural in American English. | I rested. / I took a break. |
| I went to hospital. | American English usually needs `the`. | I went to the hospital. |
| I went to company. | Non-native phrasing. | I went to work. / I went to the office. |
| I came to school. | Can sound like K-12 student language in a PhD context. | I came to campus. |
| I go to lab. | Missing article. | I go to the lab. |

## 2. Opinions, Framing, Hedging, and Confidence
| Common Korean English | Reason | Use Instead |
|---|---|---|
| According to me, this comparison is not fair. | Non-native phrasing. | In my view, this comparison is not fair. |
| As I know, this dataset is publicly available. | Non-native. | As far as I know, this dataset is publicly available. |
| As I remember, this paper used the same benchmark. | Non-native. | If I remember correctly, this paper used the same benchmark. |
| As I heard, the lab is hiring new PhD students. | Non-native. | From what I've heard, the lab is hiring new PhD students. |
| As I told you, the evaluation setting is different. | Can sound like a reprimand. | As I mentioned earlier, the evaluation setting is different. |
| In my opinion, I think this baseline is too weak. | Redundant. | I think this baseline is too weak. |
| Personally, I think we should rerun the experiment. | Often redundant. | I think we should rerun the experiment. |
| From my side, everything is ready for the meeting. | Less natural in American English. | On my end, everything is ready for the meeting. |
| In my case, I usually work better in the morning. | Often overused by Korean speakers. | I usually work better in the morning. |
| About this problem, I think we need more data. | Topic-first Korean word order. | I think the main issue is that we need more data. |
| For this problem, we increased the regularization strength. | Often sounds translated. | To address this problem, we increased the regularization strength. |
| Regarding about the evaluation setup, I have one concern. | Double marking. | Regarding the evaluation setup, I have one concern. |
| Especially the validation set is very small. | Korean-style word order. | The validation set is especially small. |
| The reason is because the model overfits the training data. | Redundant structure. | The reason is that the model overfits the training data. |
| That's why because the accuracy dropped. | Incorrect combination. | That's because the accuracy dropped after we changed the preprocessing pipeline. |
| There are many reasons. | Grammatically fine but vague and textbook-like. | There are two main reasons. |
| I have three opinions. | Sounds unnatural for discussion points. | I have three thoughts. / There are three points I'd make. |
| I have some thinkings. | Incorrect. | I have a few thoughts. |
| My thought is that the model needs more training data. | Often unnatural in discussion. | My view is that the model needs more training data. |
| My opinion is that we should submit to the next deadline. | Correct but can sound formal or repetitive. | I think we should submit to the next deadline. |
| I feel that the evaluation is not strong enough. | Can make an analytical claim sound emotional. | I think the evaluation is not strong enough. |
| I think maybe the model is overfitting. | Too much hedging. | I suspect the model is overfitting. |
| Maybe I think we should rerun the experiment. | Non-native word order. | I think we should rerun the experiment. |
| Probably maybe the data loader is causing the slowdown. | Redundant. | The data loader is probably causing the slowdown. |
| I'm not sure, but I think maybe the issue is the learning rate. | Excessive hedging weakens the point. | I suspect the issue is the learning rate. |
| Maybe I'm wrong, but the retriever could be the bottleneck. | Unnecessarily weakens your contribution. | One possibility is that the retriever is the bottleneck. |
| I guess the model memorized the training examples. | Can sound like unsupported speculation in research talk. | My hypothesis is that the model memorized the training examples. |
| I strongly believe our method is more robust. | Sounds like personal conviction rather than evidence. | The evidence suggests that our method is more robust in this setting. |
| For sure, the larger model will perform better. | Can sound overconfident. | I'm fairly confident that the larger model will perform better. |
| The new method will work 100 percent. | Too absolute in research discussion. | I'm very confident that the new method will work in this setting. |

## 3. Listening, Clarification, and Checking Understanding
| Common Korean English | Reason | Use Instead |
|---|---|---|
| I don't know. | Can sound abrupt or disengaged. | I'm not sure. |
| I don't know well. | Non-native phrasing. | I'm not very familiar with that. |
| I don't know exactly. | Slightly unnatural. | I'm not entirely sure. |
| I have no idea. | Much stronger than Korean speakers sometimes intend. | I'm not sure yet. |
| I don't understand. | Natural, but can sound abrupt in academic discussion. | I'm not sure I follow. |
| I cannot catch you. | Non-native. | I didn't catch that. |
| Say again. | Sounds like a command. | Could you say that again? |
| One more time, please. | Understandable but classroom-like. | Could you repeat that? |
| Speak slowly, please. | Sounds directive. | Could you slow down a little? |
| Your English is too fast. | Places the problem on the other speaker. | I'm having a little trouble keeping up. |
| What do you mean? | Natural but can sound confrontational depending on tone. | Could you clarify what you mean by that? |
| Explain more. | Too direct. | Could you elaborate on that? |
| Can you explain this? | Can sound like testing the person. | Could you walk me through this? |
| Do you understand? | Sounds teacher-to-student. | Does that make sense? |
| Understand? | Very commanding. | Does that make sense? |
| You don't know this benchmark? | Can sound condescending. | Have you come across this benchmark before? |
| Do you know this paper? | Understandable but slightly less natural. | Are you familiar with this paper? |
| I expected you know. | Incorrect and confrontational. | I thought you might know. |
| You should know. | Sounds accusatory. | I thought this might be relevant to your work. |
| I disagree. | Natural, but can sound abrupt in some academic settings. | I'm not sure I agree with that. |
| You are wrong. | Targets the person directly. | I don't think that's correct. |

## 4. Disagreement, Criticism, and Research Discussion
| Common Korean English | Reason | Use Instead |
|---|---|---|
| That's wrong. | Strong rejection. | I don't think that's quite right. |
| That's not true. | Can sound confrontational. | I'm not sure that's accurate. |
| I don't agree with you. | Sounds like opposition to the person. | I don't agree with that interpretation. |
| Your idea has a problem because it assumes access to labeled data. | Sounds personally critical. | One concern I have is that the idea assumes access to labeled data. |
| This method has many problems. | Too broad and negative. | There are a few limitations we should consider. |
| Your logic is strange. | Sounds insulting. | I'm not sure the argument follows from that assumption. |
| That doesn't make sense because the assumption doesn't imply the conclusion. | Natural but can sound harsh. | I'm not sure I see how that assumption leads to the conclusion. |
| It's nonsense. | Very aggressive. | I don't think the evidence supports that conclusion. |
| Why did you do that? | Can sound accusatory. | What led you to choose that approach? |
| Why didn't you use the stronger baseline? | Can sound like blame. | Was there a reason you decided not to use the stronger baseline? |
| Why is this like this? | Non-native and vague. | Why does this behave this way? |
| What is the reason? | Grammatically fine but formal and stiff. | Why is that? / What's driving this? |
| How about this problem? | Too vague. | How should we handle this issue? |
| You misunderstood me. | Places responsibility on the listener. | I don't think I explained that clearly. |
| You didn't understand my point. | Sounds blaming. | I don't think my point came across clearly. |
| You didn't listen. | Direct blame. | I may not have made that clear earlier. |
| That's not what I mean. | Can sound defensive. | Let me rephrase that. |
| That's what I said. | Can sound irritated. | Right—that's the point I was trying to make. |
| I told you. | Often sounds like “I warned you.” | That's what I was concerned about earlier. |
| Like I said, the main issue is memory usage. | Natural, but can sound annoyed. | As I mentioned earlier, the main issue is memory usage. |
| But I think the comparison is still unfair. | Repeatedly starting answers with `but` sounds adversarial. | I see your point. My concern is that the comparison may still be unfair. |
| Yes, but your baseline uses more training data. | Usually feels like rejection. | I agree that the baseline is strong. My concern is that it uses more training data. |
| No, actually, the model was trained for ten epochs. | Strong correction. | I think it's slightly different—the model was trained for ten epochs. |
| Actually, this number is wrong. | Overuse can make you sound constantly corrective. | I think this number may be incorrect. |
| Obviously, the larger model performs better. | Implies the point should already be obvious. | The results show that the larger model performs better in this setting. |
| Clearly, our method is better. | Can be too strong without evidence. | The results suggest that our method performs better in this setting. |
| As everyone knows, larger models require more memory. | Pressures the audience to already know. | It's well established that larger models generally require more memory. |
| Everybody knows that this benchmark is outdated. | Overgeneralized and dismissive. | Prior work has shown several limitations of this benchmark. |
| It is common sense. | Can sound insulting. | That seems like a reasonable assumption. |
| It's basic. | Can sound condescending. | This is a fairly standard idea. |
| It's easy. | May minimize difficulty. | This part is fairly straightforward. |
| Simple. | Can sound dismissive. | The basic idea is straightforward. |
| Just use a larger batch size. | Can trivialize the task. | One option would be to try a larger batch size. |
| You can just increase the learning rate. | Can make the other person feel the task should be obvious. | You could try increasing the learning rate. |

## 5. Requests, Email, Follow-Ups, and Apologies
| Common Korean English | Reason | Use Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Please do this. | More directive than Korean `해주세요`. | Could you do this? |
| Please check this. | Sounds like an instruction. | Could you take a look at this? |
| Please confirm that you can attend the meeting. | Formal and directive. | Could you confirm whether you can attend the meeting? |
| Please reply. | Can sound demanding. | Could you let me know when you get a chance? |
| Please send me the latest draft. | Directive. | Could you send me the latest draft? |
| Please give me feedback. | Slightly command-like. | Could you give me some feedback on this? |
| Please let me know ASAP. | Pressuring and vague. | Could you let me know by Thursday afternoon? |
| As soon as possible. | Vague deadline. | Could you send it by Friday at noon? |
| I need this today. | Sounds demanding. | Would you be able to send this today? |
| You didn't send it. | Direct blame. | I don't think I've received it yet. |
| You forgot to send me the updated figure. | Directly points out fault. | I just wanted to follow up on the updated figure. |
| Didn't you see my email? | Sounds accusatory. | Just checking whether you had a chance to see my email. |
| Why didn't you reply? | Sounds confrontational. | I wanted to follow up on my earlier message. |
| I am waiting for your reply. | Sounds demanding and non-native. | I look forward to hearing from you. |
| Thank you for your cooperation. | Bureaucratic. | Thanks for your help. |
| Kindly check. | Stiff in U.S. academic settings. | Could you take a look? |
| Please kindly review the attached draft. | Overly formal. | Could you please review the attached draft? |
| Please advise. | Formal/corporate. | Let me know what you think. |
| Sorry for bothering you. | Often over-apologetic. | Thanks for taking the time. |
| Sorry to disturb you. | Overly formal. | Do you have a minute? |
| Sorry for asking. | Makes a normal question sound inappropriate. | Thanks for clarifying. |
| Sorry for my poor English. | Self-deprecating. | I may need a second to phrase this. |
| Sorry for the stupid question, but why do we normalize this value? | Undermines your own contribution. | I may be missing something, but why do we normalize this value? |
| I'm very sorry to bother you again. | Too apologetic for a normal follow-up. | Just following up on my previous message. |

| Maybe next time. | Often sounds like a polite rejection. | I can't Friday, but I'm free next Tuesday. |

| I will do it. | Slightly robotic. | Sure, I'll take care of it. |
| I'll check it. | Vague. | I'll take a look. |
| I'll confirm and tell you. | Non-native. | I'll check and get back to you. |

| I will treat you. | Understandable but less natural. | Lunch is on me. |

| How about the experiment? | Vague. | How did the experiment go? |

| I'm fine. | Natural, but sometimes slightly distant. | I'm good, thanks. |
| I'm okay. | Can imply something is slightly wrong. | I'm doing well. |

| Everyone is better than me. | Too broad and self-defeating. | Speaking is currently my weakest area. |

| The experiment failed. | Too vague. | The experiment didn't work as expected. |
| I failed the experiment. | Personalizes the result. | The experiment didn't produce the expected result. |
| The code doesn't work. | Too vague for technical discussion. | The code fails during evaluation. |
| Something is wrong. | Provides no useful information. | The training loss diverges after epoch 20. |
| There is a problem with the experiment. | Too vague. | The main issue is that the validation loss starts increasing after epoch 20. |
| I have some issue with the training code. | Vague and slightly non-native. | I'm running into an issue with the training code during distributed evaluation. |
| The result is good. | Too vague. | Accuracy improved by 4.2 points. |

| It works well. | Too vague. | It performs consistently across all three datasets. |
| It doesn't work. | Too absolute. | It performs poorly in this setting. |
| There is no problem. | Too absolute. | We haven't observed any major issues so far. |
| There is no difference. | Can overstate statistical certainty. | We don't observe a meaningful difference. |
| Almost same. | Incomplete. | They're almost the same. |

## 10. Academic Writing, Evidence, and Technical Claims
| Common Korean English | Reason | Use Instead |
|---|---|---|
| We can know that the larger model performs better. | Non-native academic phrasing. | We can see that the larger model performs better on all three datasets. |
| We can find that accuracy improves as the dataset grows. | Often overused. | We observe that accuracy improves as the dataset grows. |
| Through this experiment, we can see the benefit of regularization. | Literal translation. | The experiment shows that regularization improves generalization. |
| Through the result, we can see that the model is more robust. | Non-native. | The results suggest that the model is more robust to distribution shift. |
| According to the experiment, our method is faster. | Awkward agency. | Based on the results, our method is faster than the baseline. |
| By this result, we can conclude that the method is effective. | Non-native. | Given these results, the method appears effective in this setting. |
| This result means our method always works better. | Sometimes too strong. | This result suggests that our method works better under the evaluated conditions. |
| We proved that our method is more robust. | Often too strong for empirical work. | Our results indicate that our method is more robust in the evaluated settings. |
| This proves our method is better. | Overgeneralized. | These results suggest that our method performs better in this setting. |
| Our method is the best. | Too broad. | Our method achieves the best performance among the evaluated baselines. |
| We beat all baselines. | Informal and competitive. | We outperform all evaluated baselines. |
| This paper is bad. | Too broad and dismissive. | I think the evaluation is the weakest part of the paper. |
| This paper is useless. | Extreme. | I'm not sure this paper addresses our setting. |
| This paper is easy. | Can trivialize the contribution. | The core idea is relatively straightforward. |
| Their method is simple. | Can sound dismissive. | The method is conceptually simple. |
| They just use a larger encoder. | `just` minimizes the contribution. | Their approach primarily relies on a larger encoder. |
| We just add another retrieval step. | Minimizes your own work. | Our approach adds an additional retrieval step before generation. |
| Basically, our method is better because it uses more data. | Overuse can make explanations sound imprecise. | Our method performs better mainly because it uses more training data. |
| Simply, we increase the context window. | Can trivialize the problem. | We increase the context window from 4K to 16K tokens. |
| It depends. | Can end the conversation too quickly. | It depends mainly on the dataset size and the available compute budget. |
| Case by case. | Fragment and non-native. | It depends on the case. |
| There are pros and cons. | Too generic. | The main tradeoff is between accuracy and inference cost. |
| Everything has pros and cons. | Avoids actual analysis. | The main tradeoff here is that the method improves accuracy but increases inference latency. |
| We need to make consensus. | Incorrect collocation. | We need to reach a consensus. |
| We need to match our opinions. | Literal Korean translation. | We need to get aligned. |
| We need to unify our opinions. | Non-native. | We need to agree on an approach. |
| I will follow your opinion. | Sounds submissive. | I'm happy to go with your suggestion. |
| I respect your opinion, but I think the comparison is unfair. | Can sound defensive or formulaic. | I see where you're coming from. My concern is that the comparison uses different training budgets. |
| Don't misunderstand me. | Signals that something harsh is coming. | Let me clarify what I mean. |
| I have no bad intention, but your presentation was confusing. | Defensive and unnatural. | I had trouble following the transition between the method and the evaluation. |
| This is advice for you: you should shorten the introduction. | Sounds hierarchical. | One suggestion would be to shorten the introduction so you can spend more time on the results. |
| I'm saying this for you. | Can sound controlling. | I thought this feedback might be useful. |

## 11. Fillers, Transitions, and Information Structure
| Common Korean English | Reason | Use Instead |
|---|---|---|
| How can I say... the result is not very convincing. | Common non-native filler. | Let me think how to phrase this—the result doesn't seem very convincing yet. |
| How to say... I think this assumption is too strong. | Incomplete. | How should I put this? I think this assumption may be too strong. |
| What should I say... maybe we need another experiment. | Can sound uncertain if overused. | Let me think for a second. I think we may need another experiment. |
| I mean... I mean... the model is not exactly failing. | Excessive repair makes speech harder to follow. | Let me restart that. The model is not failing, but its performance is unstable. |
| So... so... so... we changed the learning rate. | Overuse weakens structure. | Because the loss was unstable, we changed the learning rate. |
| And then we changed the dataset, and then we retrained the model, and then we evaluated it. | Sounds elementary when overused. | After changing the dataset, we retrained the model and then evaluated it. |
| However, the model is faster. However, it uses more memory. However, accuracy is similar. | Makes speech/writing sound overly contrastive. | The model is faster, but it uses more memory, while accuracy remains similar. |
| Therefore, we changed the optimizer. Therefore, the loss became more stable. | Sounds overly formal. | We changed the optimizer, and as a result, the loss became more stable. |
| In terms of efficiency, regarding memory usage, our model is better. | Too many introductory phrases delay the main point. | Our model uses less memory during inference. |
| Before I ask my question, let me explain the entire background of the project first. | American listeners may lose the point. | My question is whether the comparison is fair. For context, the two methods use different training budgets. |
| There are several things we tried over the last month, and after all of that, I think the retriever is the main problem. | Makes the structure feel indirect. | I think the retriever is the main bottleneck. We reached that conclusion after testing three other components. |
| Explaining a problem without asking for anything | The listener may not know what you need. | Could you help me decide between A and B? |
| What should I do? | Sounds overly dependent. | I'm deciding between A and B. Which direction seems more promising? |
| Please tell me what to do. | Sounds passive. | I'd like your input on which next step makes more sense. |
| I don't know what to do. | Too broad. | I see two possible next steps, but I'm not sure which to prioritize. |

## How to Study This File

Do not try to memorize all of the sentences at once. Choose five to ten rows per day.

1. Read only the **Common Korean English** sentence.
2. Say the **Use Instead** version from memory.
3. Say another sentence with the same pattern using your actual research.
4. Use at least one target expression in a real meeting, seminar, email, or conversation that day.
5. At the end of the day, write down one sentence that came out in the Korean-English form and repair it.

The highest-value goal is not to sound artificially indirect. It is to choose the level of directness, certainty, politeness, and specificity that matches the situation.
